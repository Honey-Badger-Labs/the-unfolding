# Science policy

The Unfolding is allowed to be beautiful and strange. It is not allowed to mislead.

## Labels

| Label | Use it when | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Observed | Telescopes have seen this kind of thing directly | A ring of light around a black hole |
| Model-dependent | It follows from observations through a model with assumptions | The first 380,000 years |
| Artistic interpretation | It is a visual metaphor or an invented bridge | Reading an eye in a nebula |

A stage can show a procedural shape and still be **Observed** if the note says the shape is procedural.

## Rules

- Every event cites at least one source in `data/sources.json`, and every source says which claim it supports.
- Dates are ranges. Future events are marked `future: true` and say "from now".
- Exaggerations are stated: "sizes are exaggerated", "the order here is a creative choice".
- The Big Bang is space expanding everywhere, never an explosion into empty space.
- The story is about matter organising itself into complexity. It never claims the universe was aiming at us.

## Known simplifications

- Planet sizes and orbital speeds are scaled for visibility.
- The cosmic web, galaxies and nebulae are procedural, not survey data or images.
- The journey follows one line of descent (web → galaxy → star → planets) and skips the many generations of stars that made the heavy elements.
- Every transition is a time-lapse: millions to billions of years pass in seconds. The cosmic clock shows how fast.
- The clock is the journey's own clock. Stages that are not tied to one epoch (the black hole, the pillars) still get a place on it.
- Fusion is drawn enormously larger and slower than real, and only its first step (two hydrogen nuclei meeting) is shown.
- Real young stars show ringed discs before hydrogen fusion fully ignites; the journey shows ignition first.
- The beginning is shown from inside: a glare that thins as space expands. There is no outside view, because there is no outside.
- In the red giant scene Earth is swallowed; its real fate is uncertain. Mars survives.
- The black hole is drawn without the bending of light round it: a flat tilted disc, a ring and a shadow. A true view would show the far side of the disc arched over the shadow.
- The galaxy merger is a real gravity calculation, simplified: Toomre and Toomre's restricted method, with a drag added near close approach to stand in for dynamical friction. The black holes' last orbits are drawn, not computed: they close in and speed up, and their spin is used to wind the new galaxy's arms. In nature arms come from density waves in the settled disc, so this link is artistic.
- The opening black screen is a stylistic choice: physics has no description of 'before' the hot dense state.
- Auroras on the giant planets shine in ultraviolet and are shown in false colour. Saturn is drawn all in blues, an exaggeration: Cassini saw its northern hemisphere blue in winter, while the rest of the planet is pale gold.
- As we leave the Moon's birth, the Earth is shown as it became hundreds of millions of years later: oceans, ice and clouds. Its continents are invented. The mix of galaxy kinds in the young cluster is illustrative; at that epoch most galaxies were clumpy and star-forming.
- Saturn is shown with the rings it has today while the planets are still forming. Cassini data suggest the rings may be only a few hundred million years old; this is debated.
- The Moon's birth is compressed: the impact, the debris disc and the gathering of the Moon play out in seconds, and the Moon is drawn at about five Earth radii (it formed closer still and has since drifted out to about sixty). Whether it gathered in hours or over centuries is not settled.
- The collapse follows the rotating-infall streamlines of Ulrich (1976) and Cassen and Moosman (1981); the clumpy streamers on top are procedural.
- The gas giant's moons are drawn far closer in than real ones, and its day is shown as about two minutes. Its moons forming from the disc round the planet is the standard model, shown without its own Atlas source.
- The web grows by the Zel'dovich approximation: matter drifts in straight lines towards the nearest filament or node, and the growth follows the clock as time^(2/3), the rate for a universe filled with matter. It is eased at the very end, and the web it arrives at is procedural, not a simulation.
- During the dark ages the unlit gas is drawn faintly so that gravity's work can be seen. In reality there was nothing to see in visible light. The first stars are lit about 100 million years in, densest knots first.
- The galaxy turns clockwise with trailing arms. Old stars orbit through the arm pattern, faster than it inside and slower outside; gas clouds, star-forming regions and young clusters are drawn riding with the arms. The halo's globular clusters are placed at random.
- Leaving the black hole, the camera pulls back to the whole galaxy and dives into a spiral arm. The real change of scale is about a hundred million to one; it is compressed into a few seconds.
- The cosmic web's growth is brought forward: structure grows steadily through the transition so it can be watched forming, where in nature most of it appears late.
- Everything in the galaxy merger turns clockwise on screen, as the galaxies orbit; the simulated discs are set to spin the same way (a prograde encounter).
